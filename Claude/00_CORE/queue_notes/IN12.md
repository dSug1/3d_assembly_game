## ⛔⛔ How this row was arrived at — and a report the owner WITHDREW

It began as two reports in sequence: *"you destroyed the rotation around the gravity axis
and orthogonal to gravity: the rotation came back to the axis of the screen view plane"*,
then *"now there are some jumps in the rotation as well"*.

The first was **investigated and did not survive**. Every part of A7 already had green
vectors — the frame is orthonormal, `up` is the world vertical, the wiring compiled — and
**none of that is the same claim as *a horizontal drag yaws about gravity***, which is what
a hand judges. So that claim was written down as a number:
[`../../../tests/a7_wiring.test.ts`](../../../tests/a7_wiring.test.ts), 14 vectors, which
compose `gravityFrame` with `screenPlaneRotation`/`screenRollRotation` and assert the axis
that comes out the far end — at level, 45° down, 72° down and on the **bottom ring**:

| drag | asserted axis | tilts |
|---|---|---|
| horizontal | `|dot(axis, worldUp)| = 1` — the world vertical | all four |
| vertical | `dot(axis, worldUp) = 0` — horizontal | all four |
| roll | `dot(axis, worldUp) = 0` — horizontal | all four |

⭐ With two counter-examples in the same file, so *"it came back to the screen axes"* is a
**distinguishable** claim rather than an impression: the **camera's** own up is ≤ 0.4
against the vertical at 72°, and the view axis is ≥ 0.9 — which is the whole argument for
A7, since rolling about a near-vertical axis is barely distinguishable from yawing.

⭐⭐ **`METHOD`, and this is the carried lesson: a composition is a thing to MEASURE, not an
emergent property.** Mistake shape 4 — *a composition nobody computed* — nearly claimed a
correct piece of work. The owner's own verdict closed it: *"it's alright: the logic is
right"*.

⚠ **The second report is real, and it is this row.**

## The defect

[`../../../src/render/scene.ts`](../../../src/render/scene.ts) feeds both rules the raw
per-event delta:

```ts
screenPlaneRotation(cur, grip.frame, s.x - grip.prev.x, s.y - grip.prev.y, radPerMm)
screenTranslation(s.x - grip.prev.x, pending ? 0 : s.y - grip.prev.y, ...)
```

⛔ **`pointerNoiseMm` = 0.761 mm, MEASURED** (`src/input/noise_meter.ts`, 2026-09-14 — the
only measured number on this project). A finger held still emits a delta on every event, so
a held object turns while nobody is moving, and a slow drag arrives as a stagger. That is
the *"jumps"*.

## ⛔⛔ THE TRAP — a hard deadband is a jump traded for a jump

Zeroing every delta below `b` and passing everything above it **unchanged** inserts a step
of exactly `b` at the crossing. Three forms, and only one of them is free:

| form | slow travel | leaving zero | cost |
|---|---|---|---|
| ⛔ hard — `|d| < b → 0`, else `d` | **lost entirely** | discontinuous, by `b` | the defect, renamed |
| ⭐ soft — `d − b·sign(d)` | **lost entirely** | continuous | every delta shrinks by `b` |
| ⭐⭐ residual — accumulate; emit and subtract when `|acc| > b` | **exact** | continuous | ≤ `b` of latency |

⭐ **Build the residual form; keep the soft one reachable and A/B them by finger.** A hand
moving 0.3 mm per event still covers its full distance under the residual form, three events
later — and covering the full distance is what makes a slow drag usable at all.

## What the vectors must say

⛔ The one that matters is **continuity, not suppression**: over a long slow drag, total
emitted travel equals the input travel to within one band. ⚠ A vector that only asserts
*"small deltas emit nothing"* **passes for all three forms above, including the broken
one** — that is mistake shape 3, an idealised fixture, written in advance.

Also: per axis, with a fixture whose horizontal drag carries vertical noise, asserting the
pitch stays zero. ⛔ **Falsify before trusting** — replace the residual with the hard form
and watch the continuity vector redden.

## ⛔ Per AXIS, never on the magnitude

`dx` yaws about **gravity** and `dy` pitches about the **horizontal** (A7). A band on the
vector's LENGTH passes a 3 mm horizontal drag intact and lets the 0.7 mm of vertical noise
riding on it pitch the object — the exact cross-talk the band exists to stop.

⚠ **ONE constant, not two.** The owner said *a* slider. x and y are the same finger on the
same glass; a band that differed between them would be a claim about the hardware that
nobody has measured.

## Scope — the edges are a decision, not an oversight

- ✅ Rule **2bis** (yaw/pitch) and rule **6** (translate).
- ⛔ **NOT** 2quinte's roll — its angle already passes through the 1€ filter
  (`src/input/one_euro.ts`), and two filters in series would be tuned against each other.
- ⛔ **NOT** A6's depth driver — `CommonDragDetector` already holds below
  `MIN_TRAVEL_NOISE_MULTIPLE × pointerNoiseMm`, and a second band there would fight it.
  ⚠ If depth still jitters after this lands, it is the same idea needing the same fix and it
  belongs in **A6**, not here.

## The number

`deadbandMm` in `gestureConfig.ts` — ⛔ **millimetres, rule 3** — with a slider and a URL
override, because `IN5` says the slider ships **with** the rule.

⭐ Its landmark is the measured noise floor, **0.761 mm**: the first tunable on this project
whose landmark was measured rather than guessed. ⚠ And `IN4` is the standing warning about
landmarks — the phantom lead's computed landmark marked the **wrong end** of its range and
the hand shipped a fifteenth of it. **A landmark tells you where a range's zero is; it does
not tell you where to stand.** Ship a range that reaches well past it in both directions.

## Device pass — what a finger closes this on

1. Hold one finger still on an object for ten seconds. ⛔ It must not turn at all.
2. Drag as slowly as the hand can manage. ⛔ It must cover the full distance, not stall.
3. Reverse slowly, several times. ⛔ No ratchet — the object returns.
4. An ordinary fast drag must feel unchanged.
