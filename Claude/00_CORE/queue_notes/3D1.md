## ⭐⭐ WATCH ITEM — on the FIRST device pass after `3D1`: does a hand ORBIT for depth, or PUSH?

⛔ **This is a question to ANSWER BY LOOKING, not a feature to build.** Raised 2026-09-15
from the owner's `TECHNIQUE_CATALOG.md` §3.2 (Z-technique / DS3). It is recorded here rather
than queued as a row because the evidence that decides it can only be gathered once —
during the first real assembly attempt, before the hand learns a workaround and stops
showing you what it wanted.

### The gap it is about

⚠ **Rule 6 is TWO DOF.** `src/input/translate.ts` returns `rightM` and `upM` — the screen
plane, and nothing else. Depth arrives only through **6bis**, which needs *a second object,
selected, with a face selected, under a second finger*. So with one object in the scene, or
before the partner is selected, or while the partner is off-frame, **a part cannot be moved
in depth at all.**

⭐ And the anchor finger in rule 6 currently carries **no information**. Since the device
overturned the `STATIONARY` latch it is read as PRESENCE, every frame: it is down or it is
not. DS3's proposal is that this finger's *relative* motion should carry depth — which
would be a change to what an existing finger means, **not a new gesture**: no new
touchpoint configuration, nothing added to §4's table.

⭐ The ambiguity the catalog spends its risk section on **cannot occur here**: pinch
requires nothing held, and §4 latches roles at press. Its own open question 8.1 is already
answered by this architecture.

### ⛔ Why it is NOT queued — the competing solution

**A free orbit is a competing answer to the same problem.** Depth is hard from glass because
motion along the view axis barely changes the picture — but a user who can orbit turns depth
into an in-plane motion by rotating forty-five degrees and pushing. **The camera may already
solve this.** DS3 earns its keep where the camera is constrained, and ours is not.

⚠ It would also modify a row that closed **2026-09-15** on the glass, whose entire feel
lives in the overshoot. Today an anchor finger that drifts does nothing; with DS3 it would
push the part away from the user.

### What to actually watch for, and what each observation means

| what the hand does when a part needs to go further away | what it means |
|---|---|
| **orbits the camera, then pushes in-plane** | ⛔ DS3 is dead. Record it and delete the candidate — the camera solved it, and the work is saved |
| **pushes at the glass with the anchor finger and nothing happens** | ⭐ build it. The gesture was reached for without being taught, which is the strongest evidence this project accepts |
| **gives up, or fights the part into place in the screen plane** | ⚠ depth is a problem but DS3 may not be the answer — reconsider 6bis's reachability first |

⛔ **Do not ask the owner "would you like depth on the anchor finger?"** That is a leading
question about a control they have never used. Watch what the hand does before it is told
anything.

⭐ This project's record on predicting hands is **three for three against the prediction** —
every gain a hand touched was raised from a guess (×3.4, ×2.3, ×2), and a computed landmark
was rejected in favour of a fifteenth of it. A prediction here is worth nothing; a look is
worth everything.

⚠ If the pass happens and nobody watches for this, **say so** rather than inferring an
answer later. An unobserved pass is not evidence either way.

## The build itself

⭐ Scope, from [`../QUEUE.md`](../QUEUE.md) and
[`../../30_OBJECTS_3D/INDEX.md`](../../30_OBJECTS_3D/INDEX.md): id, placement, connector
list, assembly tree (**parent ≠ root**), face centres, and the constraint stack attached to
an object. ⛔ Not snapping — that is `3D2`.

⛔⛔ **`3D1` is named as mistake shape 4's most likely recurrence** (a composition nobody
computed), because an assembly tree composes transforms through parent–child chains — which
cost the predecessor a from-scratch rebuild. ⭐ **Write the composite check BEFORE the
code**, the way rule 6's gain was computed before it was written:

1. **the chain in one expression** — `worldOf(child)` through an N-deep chain against an
   independently derived transform, plus a round trip and a closure property;
2. ⛔ **three objects deep on day one**, not a pair — `3D5` (*the tree has never held more
   than two objects*) stays open by construction otherwise, and two objects is exactly the
   case where parent and root coincide often enough to hide the confusion;
3. **re-rooting** — grab a child: the subtree moves, the parent stores the relative
   transform and stays static.

⚠ And two traps specific to this codebase:

* **The mesh transform will have three writers.** Today `scene.ts` composes follower
  position + sway offset, with `qHome` read back so sway never compounds. A modelled
  placement through a parent chain makes it `placement(chain) × follow × sway` — **write
  that ordering down as one expression before rewiring**, because it spans three files.
* ⛔ **`pickResult.faceId` is a TRIANGLE index, not a face.** Face selection is unbuilt, and
  rule 2 selects a *face*. The triangle → logical-face → face-centre mapping is `3D1`'s, and
  it must resolve at the render seam and hand a plain face id into `src/core`, or
  `tests/boundary.test.ts` will stop it — correctly.

## ✅ BUILT 2026-09-15 — `src/core/object_model.ts`, 42 vectors

⛔ **BUILT, NOT CLOSED.** See *"why this cannot have a device look yet"* at the end.

### What the vectors were written against, and that they can FAIL

⭐⭐ **The test file was written BEFORE the implementation**, as this row demanded, and the
three natural wrong compositions are in it as `naiveReversedOrder`, `naiveUnrotatedOffset`
and `naiveInverse` — each asserted to fail the property it violates. `METHOD`: *a test
that cannot FAIL is not a test.*

⭐⭐ **And the suite was falsified on purpose before being trusted.** With
`composePlacement` altered to add the child's offset without rotating it by the parent —
the single likeliest error, and the one that agrees with the truth for every unrotated
fixture — **14 of the 42 vectors go red**, including *preserves the world placement of
every object*, *grabbing a child moves the whole assembly*, and *attaching does not move
it*. Restored, all 42 pass. ⛔ That is the evidence the row rests on; a green suite that
was never shown to go red is not.

### The model

| | |
|---|---|
| `SceneObject` | `id`, `local` placement, `parent`, `faces`, `connectors`, `constraints` |
| `World` | a map of objects; **every operation returns a new one and mutates nothing** |
| `Face` | `id`, `centre` and outward `normal` in the LOCAL frame — §4 rule 6bis reads two centres to build `AxisBtwFaces` |

⭐ `local` is **relative to the parent** when there is one, to the world otherwise. That is
the field the glossary means by *"the parent stores the relative transform"*.

### ⭐⭐ The composition, written once

`composePlacement(parent, child)` is the one expression the whole tree is built from, and
`worldPlacementOf` folds it up the chain. Nothing else re-derives it — including
`connectorWorldPose`, which delegates to `mate_connector.worldPose` rather than
recomputing, because *a second implementation can silently disagree*.

Asserted as properties of the WHOLE chain, not of a layer: **associativity** (which is what
makes re-rooting possible at all), the **round trip** `compose(p, invert(p)) = identity` on
both sides, **involution** of the inverse, and **unit closure** of the quaternion through a
16-deep chain.

### ⭐⭐ `reroot` — parent ≠ root, and the invariant that proves it

`reroot(world, id)` re-points the edges along the path from `id` to the old root so the
held object becomes the root, **moving nothing in the world**. Each ancestor hangs off the
one below it by the inverse of what that one used to hold.

⛔ **The invariant is asserted over EVERY object, not just the one that moved** — including
a sibling that is not on the re-rooted path, and after re-rooting twice onto different
objects. ⭐ And the failure the glossary names has its own vector, by name: *grabbing a
child moves the whole assembly*, checking that the former PARENT travels by the same delta
— because *conflating parent and root means grabbing a child moves nothing*.

⚠ A rotation applied to a held child is checked too, and separately: the parent must swing
**about the child**, not about itself. A translation-only fixture cannot see that.

### ⛔ Degenerate inputs return `null`, never a default

Unknown id, a **cycle**, a **dangling parent id**, and a chain past `MAX_TREE_DEPTH` (16)
all return `null` from `worldPlacementOf`; `attach` **refuses** a cycle, a self-parenting
or an over-deep result by returning the world unchanged. ⭐ `LESSONS_CARRIED` §6 —
*a degenerate input must return `null`, never a default*: an identity placement returned
here would teleport an object to the origin, which reads as a physics bug rather than as a
broken tree.

### ⭐ `3D5` is addressed in the model, and still open in the scene

Every fixture is **three deep**, not two, and the deep-chain vector goes to 16. ⚠ That
closes *"is the model correct at depth"*. It does **not** close `3D5`, which is about the
scene having never HELD more than two objects — that needs the render wiring and a finger.

### What this row did NOT do, deliberately

* ⛔ **No snapping, no capture, no seat** — that is `3D2`, and `push`/`solve` already
  existing in `constraint_stack.ts` makes it tempting.
* ⛔ **No wiring into `scene.ts`.** Rule 2bis still drives a Babylon mesh directly; `IN3`
  attaches it to the model and adds the *empty constraint stack* precondition it is
  missing.
* ⛔ **No triangle → face mapping.** `pickResult.faceId` is a triangle index; the mapping
  is engine-side and hands a plain `FaceId` across the boundary. `IN3`/`3D4` own it.
* ⛔ **No new tunable.** The pointing tolerance the catalog's §4.1 calls for is `3D2`'s, and
  `tests/config_debt.test.ts` would refuse it here, correctly — nothing reads it yet.

### ⛔⛔ Why this cannot have a device look yet, and what that means for its status

`METHOD` closes a change with a look on a real device and nothing else. **`3D1` has no
visible behaviour**: it is a data model in `src/core` with no engine wiring, so there is
nothing for a finger to touch and a device pass would be theatre.

⭐ So the honest status is **BUILT, NOT CLOSED**, and it closes when the first thing that
USES it is looked at — which is `IN3`. ⚠ Recorded explicitly rather than quietly marking it
green, because the difference between *"vectors pass"* and *"closed"* is the whole of this
project's evidence discipline.

⭐ The DEVICE-PASS WATCH ITEM at the top of this dossier is **still pending** — it belongs to
that first `IN3` pass, not to this build.

## 🔌 WIRED 2026-09-15 — the model is AUTHORITATIVE. ⛔ Still not closed.

`scene.ts` now builds a `World` from the three boxes and every rule writes **the model**.

### ⛔ What was actually wrong before, and it is worth naming

Until this change an object's real state was `(follower.target, follower.qHome)` — an
implicit pair living **inside a display filter**. A finger wrote the filter, and the filter
WAS the truth. ⭐ That is exactly backwards: rule 6's inertia is FEEL, and feel must not be
where meaning is kept. The chain is now `SWAY ∘ FOLLOW ∘ worldPlacementOf(world, id)` for
real, re-read every frame.

### What changed, concretely

| | before | now |
|---|---|---|
| rule 6 translate | `follower.target += Δ` | writes the **model**; the follower chases it |
| 2bis / 2quinte rotate | wrote the **mesh** directly | writes the **model** |
| §1.3 rollback (`PosePort`) | snapshotted the **mesh** | snapshots the **model** |
| §2 rule 1 barycentre | mesh position **minus the sway** | the **model**, which never had sway in it |
| who writes a mesh transform | the rotation rule **and** the render loop | ⭐ **the render loop, alone** |

⭐⭐ **The held-mesh exception disappeared.** The render loop used to skip a held object
because the rotation rule wrote the mesh directly and would have been overwritten. With one
writer that special case is gone — one fewer thing to be wrong about, and it fell out of the
architecture rather than being tidied away.

⭐ **And the barycentre's sway subtraction is gone too.** It was correct but *defensive* — it
undid a contamination that can no longer happen, because the decoration never enters the
model in the first place. `display_pose.meaningfulPose` is the statement of that rule.

### ⛔⛔ NO SILENT FALLBACK TO THE MESH

The tempting `?? readPose(mesh)` was written and then **removed**. A mesh missing from the
model is a programming error, and answering it by writing the object's real pose into a
display transform — which the next frame overwrites — would make the object stop responding
for reasons nothing could explain. `requirePose` throws instead, naming the mesh.
⭐ `METHOD`: *a guard that turns a broken state into silence is worse than a failure.*

### ⛔⛔ THIS DOES NOT CLOSE `3D1`, AND IT TOUCHES A CLOSED ROW

* **`3D1` is BUILT and WIRED, not CLOSED.** Nothing here has been seen by a finger, and
  `METHOD` closes a change with a device look and nothing else.
* ⚠⚠ **RULE 6's PATH WAS REWIRED, AND RULE 6 IS CLOSED.** The arithmetic is identical —
  the same delta, the same filter, the same four tunables — but *"the arithmetic is
  identical"* is exactly the kind of claim this project has learned to distrust, and
  [`IN4`'s dossier](IN4.md) says the whole of that feel lives in the **overshoot**.
  ⛔ **Rule 6 owes a re-look on the device**, and if its feel changed, this change is why.
* ⭐ The parent chain is unexercised — three loose boxes, no assembly yet — but it is the
  SAME call, so `3D2` parenting a part changes nothing in `scene.ts`.

### What the device pass must cover

1. ⭐ **Rule 6 feels as it did** — gain, inertia, damping, lead, and above all the overshoot.
2. **Rotation still works** — free yaw/pitch and roll, and the flick rollback still restores.
3. **The orbit centre still lands where it did** — it reads a different source now.
4. **The sway still reads as one block**, not a crowd.
5. ⭐⭐ **And the DS3 watch item at the top of this file** — does a hand orbit for depth, or
   push? That is collectable exactly once.

## 🔍 DEVICE PASS 2026-09-15 — ONE DEFECT, MINE, AND A FOURTH ANSWER TO THE WATCH ITEM

⛔ **`3D1` is NOT closed.** The pass found a real defect in the wiring; it is fixed, and a
**re-test is owed**.

### ⛔⛔ THE DEFECT: the render loop drew only objects that HAPPENED to have a follower

Reported, in three parts, each sharpening the last:

> *"I orbit the camera with one finger, then with the second finger I touchpoint an object
> and try to translate it but it does not translate."*
> *"When I translate another object, the first previously selected object translates with
> one jump."*
> *"The first translated object is **locked** until a rotation or another object is selected
> for movement: it makes a jump at that time."*

⭐⭐ **That third sentence names the cause exactly.** The loop walked `followers` — a map
populated *lazily*, by whoever happened to need an entry: the sway (for the OTHER objects)
and the rotation rule (for the held one, as a side effect of storing `qHome`). While the
follower WAS the object's state this was self-consistent: a thing with no follower had no
state to draw.

⛔ **The moment the MODEL became authoritative, that stopped being true.** The wiring commit
removed `followerFor(grip.mesh)` from the translate path — it was no longer needed *there* —
and with it the only thing that gave a translated object an entry. So the model updated and
nothing drew it. The object was "locked". Then a rotation, or a sway kick from dragging
something else, finally created the entry, the next frame synced `f.target` from the model,
and everything that had accumulated arrived **in one jump**.

⭐ **Rotation hid it**, which is why the first sweep of the pass came back clean: only
translation was affected, and a hand that rotates first never sees it.

**The fix**: the loop iterates **the model's objects**, get-or-creating a follower for each.
Everything the model contains is drawn, every frame.

### ⭐⭐ THE SHAPE, which is worth more than the fix

**An implicit invariant died when the authority moved.** *"Everything that needs drawing has
a follower"* was true by construction, was never written down, and became false silently.
⛔ It is a cousin of mistake shape 4 — not a composition nobody computed, but **a
precondition nobody stated**, broken by a change that was correct everywhere it was looked
at. ⚠ Every layer of the wiring was right; the SET the loop iterated was the whole defect.

⛔ **And 409 vectors passed before and after the fix.** No vector could see it: the iteration
set lives in `src/render`, on the far side of the boundary, which is exactly why the
boundary exists and why a device look is what closes a change.

### ✅ Everything else on the pass: clean

Rule 6's feel, the rotation rules and the flick rollback, the orbit centre, the sway as a
block, and the `IN2` plumbing — all judged by finger and unchanged. ⭐ That re-confirms
**rule 6** after its path was rewired, which is the thing the wiring most needed to be told.

### ⛔ What the re-test must cover

1. **Translate an object as the FIRST thing after a page load** — the defect's exact trigger.
2. Translate a second object; the first must **not** jump.
3. Rotate, then translate, then rotate again — the paths that masked it.
4. ⭐ Rule 6's feel once more, since the loop that produces it changed again.

### ⭐⭐ THE WATCH ITEM: DS3 IS DECLINED, AND BY A ROUTE I DID NOT LIST

> *"Depth obtained by two fingers touchpoint on one object or two objects and pinch movement
> to zoom the one or two objects out."*

⛔ The table of outcomes offered three answers, and the hand gave a **fourth**: it neither
orbited nor pushed with the anchor finger — it reached for a **pinch on the object**.
⭐⭐ *A watch item is worth more than an A/B, because an A/B can only return one of the
options you thought of.* Recorded in full in [`IN4.md`](IN4.md).
