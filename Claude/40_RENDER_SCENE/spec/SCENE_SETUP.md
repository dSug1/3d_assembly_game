# SCENE SETUP — a scene's environment, lights, background, tone, contact shadows and floor

> *"update the md files when you finish to create a section about the scene setup (environment, lights, etc.) in the tiered md files"* — the
> owner, 2026-11-13

⭐ What makes a scene LOOK the way it does, where each thing is made, and how the Blender file shows the same. `Scene_2` (the engine) is the
first scene built this way. `Scene_0` and `Scene_1` keep their lights as they were (§5).

## 1. ⭐⭐ One source each

A `.glb` carries shapes and materials, and nothing of the room. Each thing is made in ONE place; the other side reads it, never retypes it.

| What | Made in | The other side |
|---|---|---|
| shapes, collision, proxies, markers, **the floor** | the scene's Blender build (`Assets/Blender/scripts/build_<scene>.py`) | the game reads the `.glb` |
| materials: colour, metallic, roughness, alpha, textures | the build: Principled's glTF inputs only (sheen, specular tint … are dropped) | the `.glb` |
| **the environment** (the panorama the PBR materials take their fill and reflections from) | ONE `.hdr` in `Assets/Environment/`, generated by `scripts/gen_sky_hdr.mjs` (no third-party image, no network) | both load the same file |
| **the lights, the background, tone mapping, contact shadows** | the scene's `lighting` in `src/content/scene_<n>.ts` — shadows, the auto-shadow switch and the frame budget depend on them | the build MIRRORS them as Blender lights, world and render settings |
| shadow maps, the floor's contour seen from below (`D196`) | the game only | Blender: not shown |

⛔ The game's look is the judge. Blender's Eevee approximates Babylon: soft shadows and reflections differ.

## 2. ⭐ The scene's `lighting` (`core/lighting.ts`)

- `background`: the clear colour, as shown. It is not tone-mapped.
- `lights[]`: in Unity's terms. Each has `type` (`SPOT` | `DIRECTIONAL`), `position` (authored units), `eulerDeg`, `intensity`, `kelvin`, `filter`, and for spots `range` and the cone angles. ⭐ Optional `softnessDeg` is the light's apparent size: its shadows are SOFT (Babylon contact-hardening, PCSS, `lighting.ts`; Blender: a sun's angle).
- `shadowStrength`: Unity's 0 – 1. In Babylon it darkens that light's share only; the environment still fills the shadow.
- `ambient`: a hemispheric fill (white above, (0.25, 0.25, 0.28) below). ⛔ Never together with an `environment` (§4): two fills burnt the floor white.
- `LightingStudio` fields (optional; a scene with a `.glb` model):
  - `environment`: the panorama's URL. Absent: the generated sky `sky_sand.hdr`.
  - `toneMapping: { exposure, contrast }`: ACES on. Blender: Filmic "Medium Contrast", its nearest.
  - `ambientOcclusion`: contact shadows (Babylon SSAO2 at half resolution; Blender: Eevee's AO). ⚠ A cost per frame.
- Sliders (☰ RENDERING): `envIntensity` (the panorama's strength, 0 – 3) and `aoOn` (the contact shadows, 0 / 1, for the device's measurement).
- Applied by `render/model_import.ts` `applyStudio()` (the environment, tone mapping, AO) before the model loads. It lives in that module, which only levels with a model load: the other levels' bundle is unchanged.

## 3. ⭐⭐ The Blender mirror (`build_engine.py`, "the GAME's lighting mirrored")

The build READS `src/content/scene_<n>.ts`'s `lighting`, `scene_1.ts`'s `unitM` and `gestureConfig.ts`'s `envIntensity`, then converts them to what Babylon does on the `.glb`'s PBR materials:

- **Positions**: the game's metres = authored × `unitM`, in Babylon's left-handed world. Babylon's glTF reader shows the file's (x, y, z) at (−x, y, z) (its root: 180° about y, z × −1). So reference = (−X, Y, Z) / the file's `unitM`.
- **A directional** of intensity I is an illuminance; Babylon's PBR applies Lambert's 1/π, as Blender does. It becomes a Blender **sun** of strength **I**, with its angle = `softnessDeg`. ⛔ It was π·I at first (Babylon assumed to have no 1/π): the game's key read π times darker than the Blender look the owner had approved.
- **A spot** has Babylon's physical falloff I/d² (d in metres; a luminous intensity) on a PBR material. It becomes a Blender **spot** of power 4π·I / `unitM`² (no spot in `Scene_2` yet: unchecked).
- **The world**: the panorama × `envIntensity`, plus the hemispheric fill × `ambient`. The panorama is MIRRORED (Mapping y × −1): Babylon reads an equirectangular image as θ = atan2(z, x) in its world (`PanoramaToCubeMapTools`), Blender as its mirror. The CAMERA sees `background`.
- **Tone**: Filmic "Medium Contrast" (exposure log2) when `toneMapping` is set, else Standard (Babylon shows the colours raw; Filmic washed the yellow floor to cream).
- **Contact shadows**: Eevee AO when `ambientOcclusion` is set.
- ⚠ **Checked once, by eye**: after the correction, the game's floor and the Blender render read alike (the owner's second look decides). Spots unchecked.

## 4. ⭐⭐ `Scene_2`'s cream studio (2026-11-13)

> *"can you recreate this atmosphere (lights, shadows of the objects and parts, environment) except the shadows coming from the light source"* → *"atmosphere_C_between_floor.png is the setup I want. build that"*

The reference was a pasta machine on a cream sculpted plinth: a high-key studio. Proposals rendered by `Assets/Blender/scripts/atmosphere_proposal.py` (renders only; it saves nothing): A (yellow floor), B (cream), **C (between: chosen)**, and C with paler wood (refused: the wood is kept).

- **Key**: `Key_softbox`, one directional, intensity **10.6** (11.8, then −5 % twice: *"reduce the 40° front light intensity by 5%"*), 4800 K, `softnessDeg` **25**, coming FROM (−0.666, 0.643, 0.378) (game world): **40°** up, **60.4° left of the BOOT view's straight ahead**, toward behind the engine (the camera at −z, yaw −90°; the owner turned it from 80.4°: *"yaw this light 15 degrees"* … *"in the other direction"*, then *"5 degrees toward the left"*). `eulerDeg` [40, 119.6, 0]. ⭐ Raised from 28.8° at 15.7 (the owner: *"I find the horizontal light a bit too strong"*): the floor as lit, a vertical face a third less. Its shadows fall long and soft toward the front-right of the boot view. No gobo (the reference's curtain shadow left out). ⛔ First placed behind-left of render C's camera, which looks from the OTHER side (+x, +z): in the game the shadow fell away from the camera (the owner's first look). Turned −136.5° about the vertical.
- **Environment**: `studio_cream.hdr` (17 KB). Cream walls at **0.45** × sRGB F2EBDF (render C's fill), the floor's ivory below, and a SOFT BOX (r 14°, ×4, warm) toward the key, laid out in Babylon's mapping so the chrome's highlight sits where the key comes from. The test checks it: the key's travel is opposite `KEY_FROM`.
- **Background**: sRGB **BFBCB7** (render C's backdrop CAC7C2, sampled; then −10 %, then +5 %). ⛔ A creamed night blue (40 % `Scene_1`'s night blue + 60 % cream, 929C9C) tried and reverted by the owner.
- **Tone mapping**: ACES, exposure **0.89**, contrast 1.1. ⭐ Exposure 1 → 0.85 and the background −10 % (the owner: *"lower the lights a little so the player can stay in the game longer"*): every light lower together, the balance kept; then *"increase the whole scene light 5%"*: 0.89, the background +5 %. **Contact shadows** on.
- **Floor**: **ivory, sRGB EFE5D0** (linear 0.864, 0.785, 0.629): halfway in linear light between `Scene_1`'s sand yellow ECDFBF and the reference's cream F2EBDF. It is designed in the Blender file (`SCENE_WORKFLOW.md` §6.3 item 1).
- **Materials** (the build): the glass is the owner's (metallic 1, roughness **0.75**, alpha 0.43); chrome C9CDD2 (metallic 1, roughness 0.14); the plinth's wood unchanged.
- Tuned once against render C: the first studio (walls 0.8, box ×6, key 3) washed the shadows out. The fill was twice render C's.
- ⭐ **The owner's first look, and the second pass** (2026-11-13). Five differences from render C:
  1. The shadow fell away from the camera: the key was placed for render C's camera, the game boots on the other side → turned onto the boot view.
  2. Everything was darker and duller (floor `D4CBB8`): the mirror's sun conversion was π too strong → the game key 5 → 15.7, the mirror × 1.
  3. The glass read as a WHITE sheet: Babylon keeps a transparent PBR material's reflections whole ("specular / radiance over alpha") → both off for every transparent material of the `.glb` (`model_import.ts`); the reflection fades with the opacity, as in Blender.
  4. The wood more orange: ACES at the low exposure (follows 2).
  5. The floor's edge and a small engine: the camera's distance (r 2.99 m), not the setup.
  - ⚠ A dark blurred blob at the top centre on the empty background: unexplained. Suspected the contact shadows (`aoOn` 0 to test).
- ⚠ **The cost**: the owner's GPU computer fell to **59 fps** with the studio (81 before the contact shadows' first settings, 145 before the studio). The contact shadows were made cheaper (8 samples, the plain blur; 16 and the wide blur before). Next to measure: `aoOn` 0 / 1, and the soft shadows (PCSS) against plain PCF. ⛔ The phone's look closes it (`FRAME_RATE.md`).

- `Scene_2` first borrowed `Scene_1`'s lights: two spots tuned for StandardMaterials, a directional and a 0.35 fill.
- On the `.glb`'s PBR materials, the chrome was BLACK, because there was no environment. The generated `sky_sand.hdr` fixed that, and was later made "less blue".
